Air quality story

Pollution follows a predictable seasonal pattern

The notebook shows that air quality gets much worse from October onward, peaking in November. The cleanest period is July to September, when monsoon rain helps clear the air.

Worst months October to January

Pollution builds as harvest activity and winter trapping both affect the air.

Cleanest months July to September

Monsoon rains naturally suppress pollution during this stretch.

What drives AQI

Fine dust and nitrogen gases matter most

AQI is most closely tied to tiny particles and traffic/combustion-related gases. The smaller and smokier the pollution, the worse the overall air score.

Data quality

Some measurements are missing a lot

Missing values are not random. A few sensors appear far less complete than the rest, so the notebook used median-based cleaning rather than forcing a false average.

Crop story

The crop dataset is rich, but the numbers are very uneven

The crop file has a broad national view with many states, districts, and crop types. It is complete enough to analyse, but area and production values are highly skewed — averages alone hide the real pattern.

Takeaway

Averages are not the best headline for this crop data. A small number of very large records pull the mean upward, so the median and spread tell a truer story.

Important caution

Limitations

The air dataset and crop dataset do not cover the same time span. They only overlap in 2015, so any link between air quality and crop output is suggestive, not definitive. The safest conclusion is that seasonal pollution is real, crop data is uneven, and both need careful interpretation.
